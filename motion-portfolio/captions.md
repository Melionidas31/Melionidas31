## LinkedIn (English)

In process safety, every review starts with the same question: how can this fail?

I studied mechanical engineering with a focus on reliability for oil and gas. When I started building machine learning systems, I noticed most models skip that question. They give you a number, but not how far you can trust it.

That's the standard I hold my own work to.

My ALOHA ML surrogate replaces dispersion simulations for real-time hazard-zone prediction. It was trained on 1,215 full-factorial scenarios and cut Red-Zone error from 22.25 m (linear baseline) to 1.82 m. The part I care about most is the out-of-distribution analysis, which shows exactly where the model stops being reliable.

AntiDeadline.ai, now in closed beta, runs a three-stage LLM pipeline that turns raw PDF, DOCX and XLSX files into root-cause analysis reports. Verified evidence, AI assumptions and missing information always sit in separate sections, so nothing reads as a confident guess.

For field operations, I built a Telegram bot that takes text, photos or voice notes from inspectors, asks follow-up questions when something is missing, and writes structured rows to Google Sheets.

I've also published on human error probability (HEART vs SLIM) and on Indonesia's energy transition.

I'm open to remote ML engineering roles, especially where a model's output feeds a safety or operations decision.
